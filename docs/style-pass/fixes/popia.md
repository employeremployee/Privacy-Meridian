## Does South Africa's Privacy Law Apply to Your Organization? (`popia-scope`)

### personalImpact

**Before**

> South Africans face a flood of unwanted marketing and a string of major data leaks. Your ID number, prized by identity thieves, sits in countless databases. POPIA covers public and private bodies handling personal information in the country. It gives that information real legal protection.

**After**

> South Africans face a flood of unwanted marketing and a string of major data leaks. Your ID number, prized by identity thieves, sits in countless databases. POPIA covers public and private bodies handling personal information in the country.

### professionalLayer

**Before**

> POPIA applies to responsible parties domiciled in South Africa, and to those abroad that use automated or non-automated means located in South Africa beyond mere forwarding. It is built on eight conditions for lawful processing: accountability, processing limitation, purpose specification, further processing limitation, information quality, openness, security safeguards, and data subject participation. Personal information also includes information about existing juristic persons, extending protection to companies. Every organization must register an information officer with the Information Regulator, and certain high-risk processing requires prior authorization. Serious offences carry a fine, up to ten years in prison, or both, and the Regulator can impose administrative fines of up to R10 million. Regulation amendments effective April 2025 made clear that an opt-out does not count as consent for direct marketing. Businesses can still market similar products to existing customers who had a chance to object. The amendments also widened the channels for objections and correction requests, and require written notice of a correction or deletion outcome within 30 days. Since April 2025, the Information Regulator requires all security compromises to be reported through its online portal.

## Moving Personal Data Out of South Africa (`popia-s72`)

### personalImpact

**Before**

> Your data may be sent to companies in other countries. Abroad, it can fall under weaker rules. POPIA allows the transfer only with comparable protection or your consent. Your safeguards travel with your data.

**After**

> Your data may be sent to companies in other countries. Abroad, it can fall under weaker rules. POPIA allows the transfer only with comparable protection or your consent.

### professionalLayer

**Before**

> Section 72 prohibits transfer of personal information to a third party in a foreign country unless one of five conditions holds: the recipient is subject to law, binding corporate rules, or a binding agreement providing substantially similar protection including onward-transfer limits; the data subject consents; the transfer is necessary for the performance of a contract with the data subject; it is necessary for a contract in the data subject's interest between the responsible party and a third party; or it is for the data subject's benefit where consent is not reasonably practicable and would likely be given. Sending special personal information or children's information to a country without adequate protection also needs the Regulator's prior authorisation, once, unless a code of conduct applies. The substantially-similar-protection test makes vendor contract review the operational core of South African transfer compliance.

**After**

> Section 72 prohibits transfer of personal information to a third party in a foreign country unless one of five conditions holds. First, the recipient is subject to law, binding corporate rules, or a binding agreement providing substantially similar protection including onward-transfer limits. Second, the data subject consents. Third, the transfer is necessary for the performance of a contract with the data subject. Fourth, it is necessary for a contract in the data subject's interest between the responsible party and a third party. Fifth, it is for the data subject's benefit where consent is not reasonably practicable and would likely be given. Sending special personal information or children's information to a country without adequate protection also needs the Regulator's prior authorization, once, unless a code of conduct applies.
