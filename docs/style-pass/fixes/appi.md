## Does Japan's Privacy Law Apply to Your Organization? (`appi-scope`)

### personalImpact

**Before**

> Japan handles data carefully, yet large leaks like the Benesse case have exposed millions of records. Your My Number and account details move through many hands. The APPI covers businesses handling data about people in Japan, including from overseas. It sets duties that match how much your data now travels.

**After**

> Japan handles data carefully, yet large leaks like the Benesse case have exposed millions of records. Your My Number and account details move through many hands. The APPI covers businesses handling data about people in Japan, including from overseas.

### professionalLayer

**Before**

> The APPI governs personal information handling business operators, applying extraterritorially to foreign operators handling personal information of individuals in Japan in connection with supplying goods or services to them. The Personal Information Protection Commission supervises the regime and can require reports, inspect, and order corrective action, with the 2020 amendments strengthening penalties and making breach reporting mandatory. Japan and the EU adopted mutual adequacy in 2019, the first such arrangement, supplemented on the Japanese side by PPC rules that apply stricter handling to EU-origin data. APPI is built on purpose specification rather than a legal-basis menu, which shapes most of its obligations. A 2026 amendment passed the Diet in July 2026 but is not yet in force. Most of it starts on a date set by cabinet order, by July 2028 at the latest. It adds an administrative surcharge equal to the gains from certain serious violations, such as unlawful third-party provision. It also adds a statistical-processing exemption aimed at AI development, parental consent for children under 16, and a dedicated biometric-data category. Entrusted processors get a direct duty to stay within the outsourced task, with some duties lifted where the contract fixes how they handle data. Tougher criminal penalties for database misuse, and a new offence for obtaining personal information by fraud or other wrongful means, apply from 17 January 2027.

## Why Organizations Are Allowed to Use Your Data in Japan (`appi-purpose`)

### articleRef

**Before**

> Articles 17-18

**After**

> Articles 17–18

### personalImpact

**Before**

> A business should not quietly repurpose the data you gave it for one thing. The APPI requires it to state a purpose and stay within it. Using your data for something new needs a fresh basis. Silent repurposing is not allowed.

**After**

> A business should not quietly repurpose the data you gave it for one thing. The APPI requires it to state a purpose and stay within it. Using your data for something new needs a fresh basis.

### professionalLayer

**Before**

> APPI does not enumerate legal bases. Operators must specify the utilization purpose as explicitly as possible, use personal information only within the scope necessary to achieve it, and obtain prior consent to exceed that scope. Acquisition must not be deceptive or wrongful, and acquiring special care-required personal information, including race, creed, social status, medical history, criminal history, crime-victim status, disability, and health-check and genetic results, requires prior consent subject to exceptions. Third-party provision generally requires consent, with an opt-out route available for non-sensitive data upon filing with the PPC. The purpose-specification architecture makes accurate, current purpose statements the backbone of Japanese compliance. The 2026 amendment (not yet in force) adds an exemption that lets operators collect publicly available sensitive data and provide personal data to third parties for statistical processing, including AI development, without consent, subject to transparency and contractual safeguards. It also defines special biometric information, such as facial-recognition data, and bars providing it through the opt-out route.

**After**

## Your Rights to Correct or Stop Use of Your Data in Japan (`appi-cease-use`)

### articleRef

**Before**

> Articles 34-35

**After**

> Articles 34–35

### personalImpact

**Before**

> A wrong record can distort the offers and decisions you receive. Once collected, data can be used in ways you never intended. The APPI lets you correct your data or ask a business to stop using it. You regain a measure of control over it.

**After**

> A wrong record can distort the offers and decisions you receive. Once collected, data can be used in ways you never intended. The APPI lets you correct your data or ask a business to stop using it.

### professionalLayer

**Before**

> Article 34 provides correction, addition, or deletion of retained personal data where contents are inaccurate, following necessary investigation. Article 35 provides cessation of use or deletion where data was handled in violation of the Act, and the 2020 amendments broadened this to cases where the operator no longer needs the data, where a reportable breach occurred, or where handling risks harming the individual's rights or legitimate interests. Operators may adopt alternative measures protecting the individual's interests where full cessation is impractical. Results must be notified without delay. The 2026 amendment (not yet in force) adds specific protections for children under 16, including a parental-consent requirement and strengthened rights to request deletion or suspension of their data.

**After**

> Article 34 provides correction, addition, or deletion of retained personal data where contents are inaccurate, following necessary investigation. Article 35 provides cessation of use or deletion where data was handled in violation of the Act, and the 2020 amendments broadened this to cases where the operator no longer needs the data, where a reportable breach occurred, or where handling risks harming the individual's rights or legitimate interests. ## Moving Personal Data Out of Japan (`appi-transfers`)

### personalImpact

**Before**

> Recent cases showed Japanese users' data being accessed from abroad. Once overseas, it may follow weaker rules. The APPI requires consent or equivalent safeguards before a transfer. Your protections travel with your data.

**After**

> Recent cases showed Japanese users' data being accessed from abroad. Once overseas, it may follow weaker rules. The APPI requires consent or equivalent safeguards before a transfer.

### professionalLayer

**Before**

> Article 28 requires prior consent to provide personal data to a third party in a foreign country, with the 2020 amendments requiring that the individual first receive information about the destination country's data protection system and the recipient's measures. Consent is unnecessary where the recipient is in a country designated by PPC rules as maintaining equivalent standards, currently the EEA states and the UK, or where the recipient maintains equivalent safeguards through contract, intra-group rules, or a recognised international certification such as APEC CBPR, in which case ongoing oversight and information duties apply. The informed-consent disclosure requirement makes destination-country transparency an operational task, not a formality.

**After**

> Article 28 requires prior consent to provide personal data to a third party in a foreign country, with the 2020 amendments requiring that the individual first receive information about the destination country's data protection system and the recipient's measures.
