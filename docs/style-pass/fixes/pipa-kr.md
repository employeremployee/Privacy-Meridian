### professionalLayer

**Before**

> PIPA applies to personal information controllers across the public and private sectors, with extraterritorial reach over foreign operators processing Korean data subjects' information in connection with offering goods or services into Korea. The 2020 amendments consolidated enforcement under an independent Personal Information Protection Commission and folded the online-sector rules into PIPA. The 2023 amendments overhauled breach notification, cross-border transfers, penalties tied to total revenue, and automated decision rights. Controllers must designate a chief privacy officer, and Korea holds an EU adequacy decision. Since September 2025, Korea recognises the EU in return. PIPA's consent granularity and enforcement intensity make it one of the most operationally demanding regimes in Asia. A 2024 enforcement decree added rules on AI-driven automated decisions and chief privacy officer qualifications, and a data-portability right began in 2025, starting with healthcare and telecoms. A 2025 amendment requires foreign operators to use their Korean subsidiary as domestic agent where one exists. A 2026 amendment, in force since 11 September 2026, raises the top administrative surcharge to 10 percent of total revenue for repeated, very large or defiant violations. It also makes the business owner the final responsible person and requires notice of possible leaks.

## What Happens When Your Data Is Compromised in Korea (`pipa-kr-breach`)

### personalImpact

**Before**

> A breach can expose your resident registration number, which is hard to change and prized by fraudsters. Silence from a company deepens the risk. PIPA requires prompt notice to you and the authorities when data is compromised. You are warned in time to act.

**After**

> A breach can expose your resident registration number, which is hard to change and prized by fraudsters. Silence from a company deepens the risk. PIPA requires prompt notice to you and the authorities when data is compromised.

### professionalLayer

**Before**

> Article 34 and its implementing decree require controllers to notify affected data subjects within 72 hours of becoming aware, unless urgent containment or force majeure requires a delay, covering the items leaked, when and how, mitigation steps, and contact points. Reports to the PIPC or its designated agency are due within 72 hours where thresholds are met, including breaches affecting 1,000 or more people, sensitive information, or unique identifiers, or where outside intrusion caused the breach. Notification may proceed in stages if details are incomplete. Since 11 September 2026, people must also be told of a possible leak within 72 hours. This applies when a leak is likely but not yet confirmed. The report to the regulator still applies only to confirmed leaks. Notices must now also explain people's rights to damages, statutory damages and dispute mediation. No report is needed if the data is recovered or deleted and the risk becomes very low. Korea previously ran some of the world's tightest breach clocks for online providers, and the unified 72-hour standard preserved that stringency across sectors.

## Moving Personal Data Out of Korea (`pipa-kr-transfers`)

### personalImpact

**Before**

> Your data may be sent to companies in other countries. Abroad, it can fall under weaker protection. PIPA requires your separate consent, or equivalent safeguards, before it goes. Your protections stay with the data across the border.

**After**

> Your data may be sent to companies in other countries. Abroad, it can fall under weaker protection. PIPA requires your separate consent, or equivalent safeguards, before it goes.

### professionalLayer

**Before**

> The 2023 amendments restructured cross-border provision under Article 28-8. Transfers are permitted with separate consent after prescribed disclosures, where required by law or treaty, where necessary for contract performance with disclosure or notification for outsourcing and storage, under PIPC-recognized certification, or to jurisdictions the PIPC designates as providing equivalent protection. The PIPC recognised the EU in September 2025. There is no standard contractual clause route. The PIPC may order suspension of transfers that violate the Act. Onward transfer duties and documented safeguards accompany each route, and consent remains the workhorse mechanism in practice.

**After**

> The 2023 amendments restructured cross-border provision under Article 28-8. Transfers are permitted with separate consent after prescribed disclosures, where required by law or treaty, where necessary for contract performance with disclosure or notification for outsourcing and storage, under PIPC-recognized certification, or to jurisdictions the PIPC designates as providing equivalent protection. The PIPC recognized the EU in September 2025. There is no standard contractual clause route. The PIPC may order suspension of transfers that violate the Act. Onward transfer duties and documented safeguards accompany each route. In practice, most transfers rely on consent.
