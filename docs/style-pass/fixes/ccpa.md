## Does California Privacy Law Apply to Your Business? (`ccpa-scope`)

### personalImpact

**Before**

> Companies you have never heard of hold detailed files on you, built from your purchases, your location, and your clicks. Many of these data brokers profit by selling that picture of your life. This law reaches larger businesses and data traders handling data about Californians, even from another state. Its protections follow your data, not the company's address.

**After**

> Companies you have never heard of hold detailed files on you, built from your purchases, your location, and your clicks. Many of these data brokers profit by selling that picture of your life. This law reaches larger businesses and data traders handling data about Californians, even from another state.

### professionalLayer

**Before**

> The CCPA as amended by the CPRA applies to for-profit businesses that do business in California, collect personal information from California residents, and meet one or more of three thresholds: annual gross revenues above $26,625,000 (the 2025 figure, adjusted for inflation every two years); annual buying, selling, or sharing of personal information of 100,000 or more consumers or households; or deriving 50% or more of annual revenues from selling or sharing consumers' personal information. Unlike GDPR, it does not apply to government entities. Most non-profits fall outside it too. But an entity that controls or is controlled by a covered business, shares its branding and receives its consumer data is covered, and that can include a non-profit. Joint ventures and businesses that voluntarily certify compliance are covered as well. The law applies to personal information of California residents regardless of where the business is located. CPRA created the California Privacy Protection Agency, known publicly as CalPrivacy since November 2025, as an enforcement body separate from the Attorney General. In 2025 the CPPA finalized new regulations on automated decision-making technology, risk assessments, and cybersecurity audits. Revisions to existing duties took effect January 1, 2026, along with risk assessments. Processing that began before 2026 needs an assessment by December 31, 2027, and submissions to the agency are due by April 1, 2028. The automated decision-making rules apply from January 1, 2027. First cybersecurity audit certifications are due between April 1, 2028 and April 1, 2030, depending on revenue.

## Your Right to Know What Is Collected About You (`ccpa-right-to-know`)

### personalImpact

**Before**

> You see an ad that feels too personal and wonder how they knew. Behind it sits a profile assembled from sources you never authorized. The Right to Know lets you ask a business exactly what it collected and who it shared it with. That disclosure brings a hidden data trail into full view.

**After**

> You see an ad that feels too personal and wonder how they knew. Behind it sits a profile assembled from sources you never authorized. The Right to Know lets you ask a business exactly what it collected and who it shared it with.

### professionalLayer

**Before**

> The Right to Know under CCPA/CPRA has two components: the right to know the categories and specific pieces of personal information collected, and the right to know the categories of sources, business purposes, and third parties with whom information is shared or sold. Businesses must respond to verifiable consumer requests covering the 12-month period preceding the request. CPRA extended this to information collected on or after January 1, 2022, unless that proves impossible or would take disproportionate effort. A business relying on that exception must explain why in detail. The duties to disclose do not apply to household data, although households count toward the 100,000 threshold.

**After**

> The Right to Know under the CCPA/CPRA has two components: the right to know the categories and specific pieces of personal information collected, and the right to know the categories of sources, business purposes, and third parties with whom information is shared or sold. Businesses must respond to verifiable consumer requests covering the 12-month period preceding the request. The CPRA extended this to information collected on or after 1 January 2022, unless that proves impossible or would take disproportionate effort. A business relying on that exception must explain why in detail. The duties to disclose do not apply to household data, although households count toward the 100,000 threshold.

## Your Right to Delete Your Information (`ccpa-right-to-delete`)

### plainSummary

**Before**

> California residents can ask businesses to delete personal information the business collected from them. From January 1, 2027, this extends to information collected about them from other sources. The business must also direct its service providers to delete that information.

**After**

> California residents can ask businesses to delete personal information the business collected from them. From 1 January 2027, this extends to information collected about them from other sources. The business must also direct its service providers to delete that information.

### personalImpact

**Before**

> Old accounts and forgotten apps keep copies of your data long after you move on. Each one is a target for the next breach. The Right to Delete lets you ask a business to erase what it holds, and to pass that request to its service providers. Less data in storage means less of you exposed.

**After**

> Old accounts and forgotten apps keep copies of your data long after you move on. Each one is a target for the next breach. The Right to Delete lets you ask a business to erase what it holds, and to pass that request to its service providers.

### professionalLayer

**Before**

> CCPA/CPRA's Right to Delete parallels GDPR's Right to Erasure but with key structural differences. Businesses must delete personal information upon a verifiable consumer request subject to eight exceptions, including completing a transaction, detecting security incidents, complying with legal obligations, and engaging in research. Critically, businesses must direct service providers and contractors to delete the consumer's information. CPRA added a requirement that businesses notify third parties to whom they sold or shared the information to delete it, unless that proves impossible or takes disproportionate effort. The right currently covers information collected from the consumer. From January 1, 2027, SB 923 extends it to information collected about the consumer, including data bought from others. A business may then keep a minimal record to make sure the data stays deleted. Online-only businesses must also offer an online request method, such as a web form.

**After**

> The CCPA/CPRA's Right to Delete parallels the GDPR's Right to Erasure but with key structural differences. Businesses must delete personal information upon a verifiable consumer request subject to eight exceptions, including completing a transaction, detecting security incidents, complying with legal obligations, and engaging in research. Businesses must direct service providers and contractors to delete the consumer's information. The CPRA added a requirement that businesses notify third parties to whom they sold or shared the information to delete it, unless that proves impossible or takes disproportionate effort. The right currently covers information collected from the consumer. From 1 January 2027, SB 923 extends it to information collected about the consumer, including data bought from others. A business may then keep a minimal record to make sure the data stays deleted. Online-only businesses must also offer an online request method, such as a web form.

**After**

## Your Right to Correct Inaccurate Information (`ccpa-right-to-correct`)

### personalImpact

**Before**

> A wrong entry in your file can quietly shape the prices and offers you are shown. It can even affect the opportunities that reach you. The Right to Correct lets you have inaccurate information fixed. Decisions about you then rest on accurate records.

**After**

> A wrong entry in your file can quietly shape the prices and offers you are shown. It can even affect the opportunities that reach you. The Right to Correct lets you have inaccurate information fixed.

### professionalLayer

**Before**

> CPRA Section 1798.106 introduced the Right to Correct, bringing California closer to GDPR's Article 16 framework. Upon a verifiable consumer request, businesses must use commercially reasonable efforts to correct inaccurate personal information. The response deadline is the same 45 days as other requests. Businesses must direct service providers and contractors to correct the information. Unlike GDPR Article 19, CPRA does not have an explicit downstream notification obligation to third parties beyond service providers and contractors.

**After**

## Your Right to Stop Your Data Being Sold or Shared (`ccpa-right-to-opt-out`)

### personalImpact

**Before**

> Much of the ad industry runs on quietly selling and sharing your data. A single visit can scatter your profile across dozens of companies. This right lets you opt out, and Global Privacy Control sends that signal automatically. You can switch off the sale of your data by default.

**After**

> Much of the ad industry runs on quietly selling and sharing your data. A single visit can scatter your profile across dozens of companies. This right lets you opt out, and Global Privacy Control sends that signal automatically.

### professionalLayer

**Before**

> CCPA introduced the Right to Opt-Out of Sale. CPRA extended this to cover sharing for cross-context behavioral advertising even without monetary consideration. Businesses must provide a clear opt-out mechanism and honor opt-outs within 15 business days. After an opt-out, the business may not sell or share the data again until the consumer consents. It must wait at least 12 months before asking for that consent. The Global Privacy Control signal must be honored as a valid opt-out. Since January 1, 2026, businesses must show whether they processed the signal. From January 1, 2027, AB 566 requires web browsers to offer a setting that sends an opt-out signal. The right to opt out has no direct GDPR equivalent. It is one of the most substantive structural differences between the two frameworks.

**After**

> The CCPA introduced the Right to Opt-Out of Sale. The CPRA extended this to cover sharing for cross-context behavioral advertising even without monetary consideration. Businesses must provide a clear opt-out mechanism and honor opt-outs within 15 business days. After an opt-out, the business may not sell or share the data again until the consumer consents. It must wait at least 12 months before asking for that consent. The Global Privacy Control signal must be honored as a valid opt-out. Since 1 January 2026, businesses must show whether they processed the signal. From 1 January 2027, AB 566 requires web browsers to offer a setting that sends an opt-out signal. The right to opt out has no direct GDPR equivalent.

## Your Right to Limit How Your Most Sensitive Data Is Used (`ccpa-right-to-limit-spi`)

### personalImpact

**Before**

> Your precise location, your health, and your account logins reveal the most about you. Businesses often use these sensitive details well beyond what you expect. This right lets you limit their use to what you actually asked for. Your most sensitive information is then kept to its intended purpose.

**After**

> Your precise location, your health, and your account logins reveal the most about you. Businesses often use these sensitive details well beyond what you expect. This right lets you limit their use to what you actually asked for.

### professionalLayer

**Before**

> CPRA Section 1798.121 gives consumers the right to direct businesses to limit the use and disclosure of sensitive personal information to uses necessary to provide the goods or services an average consumer would reasonably expect, plus a short list of permitted purposes. Sensitive personal information is a defined category. It includes Social Security, driver's license, state ID and passport numbers, account log-ins and financial account credentials, and precise geolocation. It also includes racial or ethnic origin, citizenship or immigration status, religious or philosophical beliefs, union membership, and the contents of mail, email and texts. Genetic data, biometric data, neural data, health information, and sex life or sexual orientation complete the list. Sensitive information used without the purpose of inferring characteristics about a consumer falls outside this right. Businesses that use sensitive personal information for purposes beyond the permitted uses must provide a Limit the Use of My Sensitive Personal Information link. This right has structural similarities to GDPR's special category protections under Article 9 but operates through an opt-out mechanism rather than a lawful basis requirement.

**After**
