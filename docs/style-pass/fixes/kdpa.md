## Why Organizations Are Allowed to Use Your Data in Kenya (`kdpa-s30`)

### plainSummary

**Before**

> Kenyan law permits processing with your consent or where it is necessary for a contract, a legal duty, protecting vital interests, a public task, legitimate interests, or research and statistics, a familiar list to anyone who knows GDPR.

**After**

> Kenyan law permits processing with your consent or where it is necessary for a contract, a legal duty, protecting vital interests, a public task, legitimate interests, or research and statistics, a familiar list to anyone who knows the GDPR.

### personalImpact

**Before**

> Digital lenders and apps often demand access far beyond what they need. Kenyan law requires a lawful basis, usually your consent, before using your data. That consent must be genuine, not forced to unlock a service. Uses without a valid basis are not allowed.

**After**

### professionalLayer

**Before**

> Section 30 permits processing where the data subject consents, or where processing is necessary for contract performance, compliance with a legal obligation, protection of vital interests, performance of a public task, exercise of official authority, or legitimate interests pursued by the controller or a third party, subject to the data subject's overriding rights. Historical, statistical, journalistic, literary, artistic and scientific research are also listed. The controller bears the burden of proving consent. ### plainSummary

**Before**

> You have the right to know how your data is used, to access it, to correct false or misleading information, and to have it deleted. Organizations must answer access requests within 7 days and most other requests within 14 days.

**After**

> You have the right to know how your data is used, to access it, to correct false or misleading information, and to have it deleted. Organizations must answer access requests within seven days and most other requests within 14 days.

### personalImpact

**Before**

> Lenders, telecoms, and mobile money services hold data that shapes your credit and options. What each holds is often hidden from you. This law lets you see your data, correct it, object, and have it deleted. Your information stops being a one-way mirror.

**After**

### professionalLayer

**Before**

> Section 26 sets out the core rights: to be informed of use, to access personal data in custody of a controller or processor, to object to processing, to correction of false or misleading data, and to deletion of false or misleading data. The General Regulations of 2021 operationalize requests, requiring controllers to facilitate and respond to data subject requests within prescribed short timelines and free of charge in the standard case. Access requests must be answered within 7 days. Restriction, objection, correction and deletion requests get 14 days. Portability requests get 30 days, extendable in consultation with the Data Commissioner. Rights requests denied must be explained, and complaints lie to the ODPC, which operates an accessible complaints process that has generated much of Kenya's early enforcement.

## What Happens When Your Data Is Compromised in Kenya (`kdpa-s43`)

### personalImpact

**Before**

> A breach can expose your phone records, mobile money history, and ID to fraudsters. Silence from a company deepens the danger. Kenyan law requires notice to the regulator, and to you where the risk is real. You are alerted in time to protect your money and identity.

**After**

> A breach can expose your phone records, mobile money history, and ID to fraudsters. Silence from a company deepens the danger. Kenyan law requires notice to the regulator, and to you where the risk is real.

### professionalLayer

**Before**

> Section 43 requires a controller to notify the Data Commissioner without delay and within 72 hours of becoming aware of a breach where personal data has been accessed or acquired by an unauthorized person and there is a real risk of harm to the data subject. Affected data subjects must be informed in writing within a reasonably practical period, unless identity cannot be established. Processors must notify controllers within 48 hours of becoming aware, where reasonably practicable. No notice to individuals is needed where security safeguards such as encryption were in place, and notice may be delayed for a criminal investigation. Notification content covers the nature of the breach, measures taken or planned, steps people should take, the unauthorised person's identity where known, and contact points, and delayed notifications require reasons.

**After**

> Section 43 requires a controller to notify the Data Commissioner without delay and within 72 hours of becoming aware of a breach where personal data has been accessed or acquired by an unauthorized person and there is a real risk of harm to the data subject. Affected data subjects must be informed in writing within a reasonably practical period, unless identity cannot be established. Processors must notify controllers within 48 hours of becoming aware, where reasonably practicable. No notice to individuals is needed where security safeguards such as encryption were in place, and notice may be delayed for a criminal investigation. ## Moving Personal Data Out of Kenya (`kdpa-s48`)

### articleRef

**Before**

> Sections 48-50

**After**

> Sections 48–50

### personalImpact

**Before**

> Your data may be sent to servers in other countries. Protection there is not always as strong. Kenyan law allows the transfer only with proof of adequate safeguards. Your protections stay with the data across the border.

**After**

> Your data may be sent to servers in other countries. Protection there is not always as strong. Kenyan law allows the transfer only with proof of adequate safeguards.

### professionalLayer

**Before**

> Section 48 permits cross-border transfer where the controller gives the Data Commissioner proof of appropriate safeguards, including protection through the destination's laws. It also permits transfers necessary for a contract, the public interest, legal claims, vital interests, or compelling legitimate interests. The 2021 regulations add adequacy decisions by the Data Commissioner, binding corporate rules, and consent given after being told of the risks. Section 49 covers sensitive data, which needs the data subject's consent and confirmed safeguards, and lets the Data Commissioner restrict such transfers. Section 50 lets the Cabinet Secretary require processing in Kenya to protect strategic state interests or revenue. The 2021 regulations accept a server and data centre in Kenya, or at least one serving copy stored there. The listed purposes include civil registration, elections, public-finance systems, protected computer systems, early childhood and basic education, and primary and secondary health care.

**After**
