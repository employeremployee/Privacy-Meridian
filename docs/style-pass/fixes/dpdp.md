## Does India's Privacy Law Apply to Your Organization? (`dpdp-scope`)

### personalImpact

**Before**

> India runs on digital identity and payments, from Aadhaar to UPI, alongside a flood of spam calls and messages. Your data sits at the center of it all. The DPDP Act covers companies handling digital personal data of people in India, including from abroad. It sets India's first comprehensive rules for that data.

**After**

> India runs on digital identity and payments, from Aadhaar to UPI, alongside a flood of spam calls and messages. Your data sits at the center of it all. The DPDP Act covers companies handling digital personal data of people in India, including from abroad.

### professionalLayer

**Before**

> The DPDP Act applies to digital personal data processed within India, and outside India where processing relates to offering goods or services to data principals in India. It introduces its own vocabulary: data fiduciaries determine purpose and means, data processors act on their behalf, and data principals are the individuals. The Act was passed in 2023, and the DPDP Rules were notified on 13 November 2025 with staggered commencement. The provisions setting up the Data Protection Board took effect on notification, but its members had not been appointed as of August 2026. Consent manager registration starts on 13 November 2026. The core obligations and rights in Sections 3 to 17, covering notice, breach reporting, children's verification, and individual rights, start on 13 May 2027, along with the Board's powers and penalties. Until then, the older IT Act rules on sensitive personal data still apply, and CERT-In requires many organizations to report cyber incidents within six hours. Significant data fiduciaries, designated by government notification, carry added duties including an India-based DPO, audits, and periodic impact assessments. Penalties are steep, reaching 250 crore rupees for failures of security safeguards. Notably, the Act applies one uniform standard to all personal data, with no separate sensitive-data category.

## Why Organizations Are Allowed to Use Your Data in India (`dpdp-consent`)

### articleRef

**Before**

> Sections 4-7

**After**

> Sections 4–7

### professionalLayer

**Before**

> Section 4 permits processing only for a lawful purpose with the data principal's consent or for certain legitimate uses. Consent must be free, specific, informed, unconditional, and unambiguous, given through clear affirmative action, preceded by an itemized notice, and as easy to withdraw as to give. Consent managers, registered platforms through which principals can give and manage consent, are a distinctive DPDP innovation. The legitimate uses under Section 7 include voluntary provision for a specified purpose, state functions and benefits, disclosures required by law, compliance with court orders, medical emergencies, and employment-related purposes. There is no legitimate-interests basis and no contract basis, making India's menu one of the narrowest among major laws. Children under eighteen, and people with disabilities who have a lawful guardian, require verifiable consent from a parent or guardian. Tracking, behavioral monitoring, and targeted advertising directed at children are prohibited. The Rules exempt some classes, such as healthcare providers and schools, and some purposes, such as a child's safety. These duties apply from 13 May 2027.

## What Happens When Your Data Is Compromised in India (`dpdp-breach`)

### personalImpact

**Before**

> A breach can expose your Aadhaar-linked details and payment data to fraud. A company that stays silent leaves you exposed. The DPDP Act requires notice to you and the Data Protection Board when data is compromised. You learn of the risk in time to respond.

**After**

> A breach can expose your Aadhaar-linked details and payment data to fraud. A company that stays silent leaves you exposed. The DPDP Act requires notice to you and the Data Protection Board when data is compromised.

### professionalLayer

**Before**

> Section 8(6) requires a data fiduciary, in the event of a personal data breach, to give intimation to the Board and each affected data principal. The DPDP Rules operationalize this with prompt notification to affected principals describing the breach, consequences, and mitigation, an initial intimation to the Board without delay, and detailed particulars to the Board within 72 hours of awareness, extendable on request. Strikingly, the duty has no risk threshold: all personal data breaches are notifiable, unlike GDPR's risk-based filter. Combined with penalties up to 250 crore rupees for security failures and 200 crore rupees for failing to notify, breach handling is the DPDP regime's sharpest edge. These duties apply from 13 May 2027. Until then, CERT-In rules require many organizations to report cyber incidents within six hours.

**After**
