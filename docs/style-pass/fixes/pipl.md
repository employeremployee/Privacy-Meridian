## Does China's Privacy Law Apply to Your Organization? (`pipl-scope`)

### personalImpact

**Before**

> Facial recognition, super-apps, and QR codes now run through daily life in China. Each scan and tap adds to a detailed record of where you go and what you do. **After**

### professionalLayer

**Before**

> PIPL applies to processing of personal information of natural persons within the territory of the People's Republic of China. It also applies extraterritorially where the purpose is to provide products or services to individuals within China, the purpose is to analyze or assess the behavior of individuals within China, or other circumstances prescribed by law. Organizations outside China that fall within PIPL's scope must designate a representative or establish a dedicated entity within China responsible for PIPL compliance matters. PIPL operates alongside the Cybersecurity Law and the Data Security Law, creating an interlocking regulatory framework that requires holistic assessment. It is structurally inspired by but substantively distinct from GDPR, with significant differences in data localization requirements, consent standards, and cross-border transfer mechanisms.

## Why Organizations Are Allowed to Process Your Information in China (`pipl-art13`)

### professionalLayer

**Before**

> Article 13 sets out seven lawful bases for processing under PIPL. They are individual consent, necessity for a contract with the individual or for human-resources management, and necessity for statutory duties. Others cover public health emergencies or protecting life and property, and news reporting or public-opinion oversight in the public interest, within reasonable scope. The last two are publicly disclosed personal information within reasonable scope, and other cases set by law. Consent is the primary and most common basis under PIPL. Unlike GDPR, PIPL has no basis for legitimate interests. Separate consent is required in specific cases: sharing with another handler, publishing data, processing sensitive personal information, and sending data abroad. A new purpose needs fresh consent, and a handler cannot refuse service because someone declines processing it does not need.

## Your Rights When Algorithms Make Decisions About You (`pipl-art24`)

### professionalLayer

**Before**

> Article 24 requires personal information handlers using automated decision-making to ensure transparency and fairness and to avoid unreasonable differential treatment of individuals in transaction prices or other transaction conditions. For marketing and information push using automated decision-making, handlers must offer options not targeted to personal characteristics, or an easy way to refuse. For decisions with a major impact on a person's rights and interests, the person may demand an explanation and refuse decisions made solely by automated means. The anti-differential pricing provision has no direct GDPR equivalent. This goes beyond GDPR Article 22 in some respects, but PIPL sets no human-review requirement.

**After**

> Article 24 requires personal information handlers using automated decision-making to ensure transparency and fairness and to avoid unreasonable differential treatment of individuals in transaction prices or other transaction conditions. ## Moving Personal Information outside China (`pipl-art38-43`)

### articleRef

**Before**

> Articles 38-43

**After**

> Articles 38–43

### surfaceLabel

**Before**

> Moving Personal Information Outside China

**After**

> Moving Personal Information outside China

### professionalLayer

**Before**

> Article 38 sets out four routes for cross-border transfer: passing a CAC security assessment, obtaining personal information protection certification from a professional institution, signing the CAC standard contract, or meeting other conditions set by laws, regulations or the CAC. Treaties and international agreements can also govern transfers. The individual's separate consent is required in addition to one of these routes. These mechanisms differ fundamentally from GDPR's transfer framework. There is no adequacy decision mechanism equivalent, no Binding Corporate Rules mechanism, and the CAC security assessment process is government-administered rather than self-certifying. This is the most substantive structural difference from GDPR in the PIPL framework. In March 2024 the CAC relaxed these rules through the Provisions on Promoting and Regulating Cross-Border Data Flows, raising the thresholds. A security assessment is now needed for critical operators, important data, or transfers since 1 January of a year covering 1 million or more people's non-sensitive data or 10,000 or more people's sensitive data. Smaller volumes use the standard contract or certification. Transfers needed for a contract with the individual, cross-border human resources, or emergencies are exempt, as are small transfers of non-sensitive data by non-critical handlers. Certification has been a route since 2021, and detailed certification measures took effect on 1 January 2026. The mechanisms remain government-administered, but the compliance burden on routine transfers is now lighter than when PIPL first took effect.

## What Happens When Personal Information Is Compromised in China (`pipl-art57`)

### personalImpact

**Before**

> A leak can expose the accounts tied to your WeChat, Alipay, and bank. Silence from the company only widens the harm. Article 57 requires prompt action and notice when your data is at risk. You are informed in time to secure your accounts.

**After**

> A leak can expose the accounts tied to your WeChat, Alipay, and bank. Silence from the company only widens the harm. Article 57 requires prompt action and notice when your data is at risk.

### professionalLayer

**Before**

> Article 57 applies when a personal information leak, tampering, or loss occurs or may have occurred. Handlers must immediately take remedial measures and notify the relevant authorities and affected individuals. PIPL does not specify a fixed hour threshold. Separate incident-reporting measures have applied since 1 November 2025. They cover cybersecurity incidents rated relatively major or worse. A leak affecting 1 million or more people counts as relatively major. Critical infrastructure operators report within 1 hour to their protection department and the police. Central and state bodies report to their own department within 2 hours. Other operators report to the provincial CAC office within 4 hours. Where the handler's measures effectively avoid harm, it need not notify individuals, although the authorities may still require it.

**After**

> Article 57 applies when a personal information leak, tampering, or loss occurs or may have occurred. Handlers must immediately take remedial measures and notify the relevant authorities and affected individuals. The PIPL does not specify a fixed hour threshold. Separate incident-reporting measures have applied since 1 November 2025. They cover cybersecurity incidents rated relatively major or worse. A leak affecting 1 million or more people counts as relatively major. Critical infrastructure operators report within 1 hour to their protection department and the police. Central and state bodies report to their own department within 2 hours. Other operators report to the provincial CAC office within 4 hours. Where the handler's measures effectively avoid harm, it need not notify individuals, although the authorities may still require it.
