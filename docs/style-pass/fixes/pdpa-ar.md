## Does Argentina's Privacy Law Apply to Your Organization? (`pdpa-ar-scope`)

### personalImpact

**Before**

> In Argentina, control over your own data is rooted in the constitution as habeas data. Yet your details still fill credit databases and marketing lists. This law covers files and databases holding personal data in Argentina. It turns that constitutional promise into everyday rules.

**After**

> In Argentina, control over your own data is rooted in the constitution as habeas data. Yet your details still fill credit databases and marketing lists. This law covers files and databases holding personal data in Argentina.

### professionalLayer

**Before**

> Law 25,326 is one of Latin America's oldest comprehensive data protection laws, implementing the constitutional habeas data guarantee. It applies to personal data recorded in public and private files, registers, and databases within Argentina. The Agency for Access to Public Information supervises compliance. Argentina holds an EU adequacy decision dating from 2003, one of the earliest granted. The European Commission's January 2024 review kept it in place. Argentina is a party to the Council of Europe's Convention 108 and ratified its modernised version, known as 108+, in April 2023. The law predates the GDPR era, and its database-registration model and shorter response deadlines reflect that earlier generation. Modernization bills aligning Argentina more closely with GDPR have been introduced repeatedly but not yet enacted, so the 2000 framework remains the binding law.

## What Happens When Your Data Is Compromised in Argentina (`pdpa-ar-breach`)

### professionalLayer

**Before**

> Law 25,326 predates breach notification as a standard feature and imposes only general security obligations. AAIP Resolution 47/2018 recommends, as part of its recommended security measures, that incidents be documented and reported to the AAIP, and notification is treated as good practice evidencing compliance rather than a hard statutory duty. AAIP inspectors also check whether a controller has a system for notifying the AAIP and affected people (Resolution 332/2020). Sectoral rules may impose their own incident duties. Pending modernization bills would introduce a mandatory notification regime aligned with international practice, so this is an area to monitor closely.

## Moving Personal Data Out of Argentina (`pdpa-ar-transfers`)

### personalImpact

**Before**

> Your data may be sent to companies in other countries. Some of them offer weaker protection than Argentina. This law allows the transfer only with adequate safeguards or your consent. Your protections do not end at the border.

**After**

> Your data may be sent to companies in other countries. Some of them offer weaker protection than Argentina. This law allows the transfer only with adequate safeguards or your consent.

### professionalLayer

**Before**

> Article 12 prohibits transfers to countries or international bodies that do not provide adequate levels of protection, subject to exceptions in the law. They cover international judicial cooperation, medical data exchange needed for treatment or epidemiological research, and bank and stock-exchange transfers. Treaty-based transfers and intelligence cooperation against organised crime and terrorism are also exempt. The implementing decree adds the person's express consent as a further exception. The AAIP maintains a list of jurisdictions it deems adequate, broadly tracking the EU adequacy map, and has approved model contractual clauses for transfers to non-adequate destinations. It has also set guidelines for binding corporate rules within corporate groups. Argentina itself holds EU adequacy, keeping EU-to-Argentina flows unrestricted, a status tied to maintaining its protection standards.
