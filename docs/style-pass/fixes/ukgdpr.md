## Does UK Privacy Law Apply to Your Organization? (`ukgdpr-scope`)

### personalImpact

**Before**

> Your shopping, browsing, and location feed profiles that set your prices and target your ads. Companies across the UK and beyond trade that data every day. UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018 cover UK organizations, and foreign ones that target or monitor people in the UK. Those companies must follow clear rules, wherever they are based.

**After**

> Your shopping, browsing, and location feed profiles that set your prices and target your ads. Companies across the UK and beyond trade that data every day. UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018 cover UK organizations, and foreign ones that target or monitor people in the UK.

### professionalLayer

**Before**

> The UK GDPR, together with the Data Protection Act 2018, is the assimilated version of the EU GDPR that took effect at the end of the Brexit transition period. It began as a mirror of the EU regulation, with the same legal bases, data subject rights and accountability obligations. Territorial scope covers UK establishments and organizations outside the UK that offer goods or services to, or monitor the behavior of, people in the UK. The regimes have since diverged with the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025, whose data protection provisions took effect mostly from 5 February 2026. It adds a seventh lawful basis of recognised legitimate interests and replaces the Article 22 automated-decision rules with new Articles 22A to 22D. It also changes subject access, relaxes some cookie-consent requirements, and sets a data protection test for international transfers. UK compliance can no longer be assumed from EU GDPR compliance. Since 30 September 2026, the Information Commission enforces the law, replacing the Information Commissioner.

## Why Organizations Are Allowed to Use Your Data in the UK (`ukgdpr-art6`)

### personalImpact

**Before**

> Unwanted marketing calls and texts are a daily nuisance across the UK. Behind many of them sits data used without any proper reason. The law requires a lawful basis before a company can process your data at all. Separate rules, known as PECR, also limit marketing calls, texts and emails.

**After**

> Unwanted marketing calls and texts are a daily nuisance across the UK. Behind many of them sits data used without any proper reason. The law requires a lawful basis before a company can process your data at all. Separate rules, known as PECR, also limit marketing calls, texts, and emails.

### professionalLayer

**Before**

> Article 6 of the UK GDPR keeps the six lawful bases of its EU parent: consent, contract performance, legal obligation, vital interests, public task, and legitimate interests. The ICO publishes detailed guidance on selecting and documenting a basis, including an interactive lawful basis tool. Since 5 February 2026, it adds a seventh basis: recognised legitimate interests. These need no balancing test, but the processing must meet a condition in Annex 1. The conditions cover public-task disclosures, national security and defence, emergencies, crime, and safeguarding vulnerable people. Public authorities cannot use this basis, or ordinary legitimate interests, for their tasks. As under EU GDPR, the basis must be settled before processing begins. You must record which basis you rely on, and the regulator's guidance expects your record of processing to include or link to it.

**After**

## Your Right to Delete Your Data in the UK (`ukgdpr-art17`)

### personalImpact

**Before**

> An old address, a past dispute, or a mistaken record can follow you for years. Left in place, it keeps surfacing in searches and decisions. This right lets you ask for data that is no longer needed to be erased. You regain a say over what stays on file.

**After**

> An old address, a past dispute, or a mistaken record can follow you for years. Left in place, it keeps surfacing in searches and decisions. This right lets you ask for data that is no longer needed to be erased.

### professionalLayer

**Before**

> Article 17 of the UK GDPR mirrors the EU right to erasure: deletion is required where data is no longer necessary, consent is withdrawn without another basis, the individual objects without overriding grounds or objects to direct marketing, processing was unlawful, a legal obligation requires it, or the data was collected for online services offered to a child. Since 31 March 2026, it also covers data processed because of a malicious allegation. This applies across the UK. Malicious has a narrow meaning here. The accuser must have been convicted of stalking, harassment or a similar listed offence against the person. Or the accuser must be under a stalking protection order that protects them. The controller must also have investigated and decided to take no further action. Refusal grounds include freedom of expression, legal obligations and public tasks, public health, archiving and research, and legal claims. Organizations should be able to show why they refused a request. The regulator's guidance expects backup data to be put beyond use, even if it cannot be overwritten at once.

**After**
