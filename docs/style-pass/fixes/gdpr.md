### professionalLayer

**Before**

> Articles 13 and 14 establish the transparency obligation that underpins the entire GDPR framework. Article 13 applies when personal data is collected directly from the data subject. Article 14 applies when data is obtained indirectly. Article 13 information is due when the data is obtained. Under Article 14, it is due within a reasonable period and at the latest within one month. It is due sooner at the first communication with the person or the first disclosure to another recipient, whichever comes first. Required disclosures include the identity and contact details of the controller, the purposes and legal basis for processing, any legitimate interests relied upon, recipients or categories of recipients, international transfer safeguards, retention periods, and the existence of all data subject rights. ## Your Right to Correct Your Data (`gdpr-art16`)

### personalImpact

**Before**

> One wrong entry can quietly upend your life. A mistaken default, a mixed-up record, a debt that was never yours. It can cost you a loan, an apartment, or a job, and no one tells you why. Article 16 lets you require the correction, so decisions rest on accurate information.

**After**

> One wrong entry can quietly upend your life. A mistaken default, a mixed-up record, a debt that was never yours. It can cost you a loan, an apartment, or a job, and no one tells you why. Article 16 lets you require the correction.

### professionalLayer

**Before**

> Article 16 requires controllers to correct inaccurate personal data and complete incomplete data upon a data subject's request. The obligation extends beyond factual errors. An incomplete record that creates a misleading picture of an individual is also subject to rectification. Where corrected data has been shared with third parties, the controller must notify those recipients under Article 19 unless doing so is impossible or disproportionately burdensome. This downstream notification obligation is among the most frequently overlooked aspects of Article 16 compliance.

**After**

> Article 16 requires controllers to correct inaccurate personal data and complete incomplete data on a data subject's request. The obligation extends beyond factual errors. An incomplete record that creates a misleading picture of an individual is also subject to rectification. Where corrected data has been shared with third parties, the controller must notify those recipients under Article 19 unless doing so is impossible or disproportionately burdensome.

## Your Right to Delete Your Data (`gdpr-art17`)

### plainSummary

**Before**

> You have the right to ask a company to permanently delete your personal information in certain circumstances. Companies may refuse in some cases, such as a legal duty to keep the data or the defence of legal claims.

**After**

> You have the right to ask a company to permanently delete your personal information in certain circumstances. Companies may refuse in some cases, such as a legal duty to keep the data or the defense of legal claims.

### personalImpact

**Before**

> An old post, a leaked photo, an address a stalker could use. Years on, they still surface in a search and shape how people judge you. Article 17, the right to be forgotten, lets you request that data be erased. You regain a say over what remains attached to your name.

**After**

> An old post, a leaked photo, an address a stalker could use. Years on, they still surface in a search and shape how people judge you. Article 17, the right to be forgotten, lets you request that data be erased.

### professionalLayer

**Before**

> Article 17 requires erasure without undue delay when one of six grounds applies: the data is no longer necessary for the purpose collected; consent is withdrawn and no other legal basis applies; the data subject objects under Article 21(1) and no overriding legitimate grounds exist, or objects to direct marketing under Article 21(2); processing was unlawful; a legal obligation requires erasure; or the data relates to a child's information society services consent. Erasure is not absolute. Controllers may refuse where retention is necessary for freedom of expression and information, or for a legal obligation or public task. Public health is another ground. So is archiving, research or statistics, where erasure would seriously impair that work. The last ground is the establishment, exercise or defence of legal claims. Documenting refusal grounds is as operationally important as fulfilling requests. Backup and archive erasure presents a specific technical challenge that regulators increasingly expect organizations to address explicitly.

**After**

> Article 17 requires erasure without undue delay when one of six grounds applies. First, the data is no longer necessary for the purpose collected. Second, consent is withdrawn and no other legal basis applies. Third, the data subject objects under Article 21(1) and no overriding legitimate grounds exist, or objects to direct marketing under Article 21(2). Fourth, the processing was unlawful. Fifth, a legal obligation requires erasure. Sixth, the data relates to a child's information society services consent. Erasure is not absolute. Controllers may refuse where retention is necessary for freedom of expression and information, or for a legal obligation or public task. Public health is another ground. So is archiving, research, or statistics, where erasure would seriously impair that work. The last ground is the establishment, exercise, or defense of legal claims. Documenting refusal grounds is as operationally important as fulfilling requests. Backup and archive erasure presents a specific technical challenge that regulators increasingly expect organizations to address explicitly.

## Your Right to Limit How Your Data Is Used (`gdpr-art18`)

### plainSummary

**Before**

> In certain situations you can ask a company to temporarily stop using your personal information, even if they do not have to delete it. This gives you a way to pause processing while a dispute or legal matter is resolved.

**After**

> In certain situations you can ask a company to temporarily stop using your personal information, even if it does not have to delete it. This gives you a way to pause processing while a dispute or legal matter is resolved.

### personalImpact

**Before**

> You catch a company using your data in a way that feels wrong. Normally it keeps going while you argue. In some cases, Article 18 lets you freeze the processing instead. These include disputed accuracy, unlawful use and a pending objection. It then pauses until the question is resolved.

**After**

> You catch a company using your data in a way that feels wrong. Normally it keeps going while you argue. In some cases, Article 18 lets you freeze the processing instead. These include disputed accuracy, unlawful use, and a pending objection.

### professionalLayer

**Before**

> Article 18 allows data subjects to obtain restriction of processing in four circumstances: the accuracy of the data is contested and verification is pending; processing is unlawful but the data subject opposes erasure; the controller no longer needs the data but the data subject requires it for legal claims; or the data subject has objected under Article 21 and verification of overriding grounds is pending. When processing is restricted, data may only be stored, not actively processed, except with the data subject's consent, for legal claims, for protection of another person's rights, or for important public interest reasons. The controller must notify the data subject before lifting a restriction. Restriction requires technical capability to flag and isolate individual records from active processing workflows.

**After**

> Article 18 allows data subjects to obtain restriction of processing in four circumstances. First, the accuracy of the data is contested and verification is pending. Second, processing is unlawful but the data subject opposes erasure. Third, the controller no longer needs the data but the data subject requires it for legal claims. Fourth, the data subject has objected under Article 21 and verification of overriding grounds is pending. When processing is restricted, data may only be stored, not actively processed, except with the data subject's consent, for legal claims, for protection of another person's rights, or for important public interest reasons. The controller must notify the data subject before lifting a restriction. Restriction requires technical capability to flag and isolate individual records from active processing workflows.

## The Company's Duty to Tell Others When Your Data Changes (`gdpr-art19`)

### plainSummary

**Before**

> When a company corrects, deletes, or restricts your personal information, they must inform anyone else they previously shared it with, unless that is impossible or would require disproportionate effort.

**After**

> When a company corrects, deletes, or restricts your personal information, it must tell everyone it previously shared the data with, unless that is impossible or would take disproportionate effort.

### personalImpact

**Before**

> You fix a wrong record, but the company already shared it widely. The old error lives on in systems you cannot see. Article 19 makes it tell every recipient to update or delete it too. A single request reaches the copies you could not track yourself.

**After**

> You fix a wrong record, but the company already shared it widely. The old error lives on in systems you cannot see. Article 19 makes it tell every recipient to update or delete it too.

### professionalLayer

**Before**

> Article 19 is a supporting obligation triggered by three data subject rights: rectification (Article 16), erasure (Article 17), and restriction of processing (Article 18). When any of these actions are taken, the controller must communicate the action to each recipient to whom the data was disclosed, unless notification is impossible or involves disproportionate effort. Controllers must inform the data subject of those recipients upon request. Article 19 compliance depends entirely on the quality of a controller's data sharing records. Organizations that cannot identify who received personal data cannot fulfill this obligation. This makes Article 19 a practical test of data mapping maturity.

**After**

> Article 19 is a supporting obligation triggered by three data subject rights: rectification (Article 16), erasure (Article 17), and restriction of processing (Article 18). When any of these actions are taken, the controller must communicate the action to each recipient to whom the data was disclosed, unless notification is impossible or involves disproportionate effort. Organizations that cannot identify who received personal data cannot fulfill this obligation.

## Your Right Not to Be Judged by an Algorithm Alone (`gdpr-art22`)

### professionalLayer

**Before**

> Article 22(1) sets a general prohibition on decisions based solely on automated processing, including profiling, that produce legal or similarly significant effects. The Court of Justice confirmed this reading in SCHUFA (C-634/21, 2023). Three conditions must all be met: there is a decision, it is based solely on automated processing, and it has legal or similarly significant effects. Exceptions exist where the decision is necessary for a contract, authorized by law, or based on explicit consent. For the contract and consent exceptions, the controller must at least offer the data subject human intervention, a chance to express a view, and a way to contest the decision. For decisions authorized by law, that law must set the safeguards. The practical challenge is defining meaningful human involvement. Rubber-stamping automated outputs without genuine review does not count.

**After**

> Article 22(1) sets a general prohibition on decisions based solely on automated processing, including profiling, that produce legal or similarly significant effects. The Court of Justice confirmed this reading in SCHUFA (Case C-634/21, 2023).
