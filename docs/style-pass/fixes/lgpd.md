## Does Brazil's Privacy Law Apply to Your Organization? (`lgpd-scope`)

### personalImpact

**Before**

> Massive leaks have exposed the CPF numbers and records of millions of Brazilians. That data fuels fraud, spam, and unfair profiling. This law sets binding rules for almost any organization handling data of people in Brazil. Your CPF and daily habits gain real legal protection.

**After**

> Massive leaks have exposed the CPF numbers and records of millions of Brazilians. That data fuels fraud, spam, and unfair profiling. This law sets binding rules for almost any organization handling data of people in Brazil.

### professionalLayer

**Before**

> LGPD applies to any natural or legal person, public or private, that carries out processing operations of personal data of individuals located in Brazil, regardless of the nationality or country of headquarters of the organization. Territorial scope also covers processing that takes place in Brazil or processing of data collected in Brazil. LGPD is broadly GDPR-aligned in structure. It establishes ten legal bases for processing, a set of data subject rights, requirements for a data protection officer called the Encarregado, impact reports when the ANPD asks for them, breach notification, and international transfer mechanisms. Key structural differences from GDPR include a broader set of legal bases and a different treatment of legitimate interests. A February 2026 law made the ANPD a national regulatory agency, the Agência Nacional de Proteção de Dados. In August 2026 it issued its first large fine, R$153.7 million against ByteDance, TikTok's owner, over children's data. The deadline for an appeal to the ANPD board has passed. Brazil's standard contractual clauses took effect in August 2024, and contracts had to adopt them by August 2025. In January 2026, Brazil and the EU recognised each other as giving adequate protection.

**After**

> The LGPD applies to any natural or legal person, public or private, that carries out processing operations of personal data of individuals located in Brazil, regardless of the nationality or country of headquarters of the organization. Territorial scope also covers processing that takes place in Brazil or processing of data collected in Brazil. The LGPD is broadly GDPR-aligned in structure. It establishes 10 legal bases for processing, a set of data subject rights, requirements for a data protection officer called the Encarregado, impact reports when the ANPD asks for them, breach notification, and international transfer mechanisms. Key structural differences from GDPR include a broader set of legal bases and a different treatment of legitimate interests. A February 2026 law made the ANPD a national regulatory agency, the Agência Nacional de Proteção de Dados. In August 2026 it issued its first large fine, R$153.7 million against ByteDance, TikTok's owner, over children's data. The deadline for an appeal to the ANPD board has passed. Brazil's standard contractual clauses took effect in August 2024, and contracts had to adopt them by August 2025. In January 2026, Brazil and the EU recognized each other as giving adequate protection.

## Why a Company Is Allowed to Use Your Information in Brazil (`lgpd-art7`)

### plainSummary

**Before**

> Like GDPR, Brazil's privacy law requires companies to have a valid legal reason to process your personal information. Brazil's law provides ten possible reasons, more than GDPR's six, including some that are unique to Brazil's legal and social context.

**After**

> Like the GDPR, Brazil's privacy law requires companies to have a valid legal reason to process your personal information. Brazil's law provides 10 possible reasons, more than the GDPR's six, including some that are unique to Brazil's legal and social context.

### personalImpact

**Before**

> A company holding your data cannot simply use it however it likes. Every use is meant to rest on a specific, lawful reason. Article 7 sets out the limited grounds, such as your consent or a contract. Uses that fit none of them are not permitted.

**After**

> A company holding your data cannot simply use it however it likes. Every use is meant to rest on a specific, lawful reason. Article 7 sets out the limited grounds, such as your consent or a contract.

### professionalLayer

**Before**

> Article 7 establishes ten lawful bases for processing personal data under LGPD: consent; compliance with a legal or regulatory obligation; execution of public policies; research by a research body; execution of a contract or pre-contractual procedures; exercise of rights in judicial, administrative, or arbitration proceedings; protection of life or physical safety; health protection by health professionals or entities; legitimate interests of the controller or third parties; and credit protection. Legitimate interests need a balancing test, and Article 10 adds purpose, necessity and transparency conditions. The ANPD set out its approach in a February 2024 guide. Legitimate interests cannot justify processing sensitive data. Credit protection is a standalone basis, reflecting Brazil's credit bureau ecosystem.

**After**

> Article 7 establishes 10 lawful bases for processing personal data under the LGPD. They are consent, compliance with a legal or regulatory obligation, execution of public policies, research by a research body, and execution of a contract or pre-contractual procedures. They also include the exercise of rights in judicial, administrative, or arbitration proceedings, protection of life or physical safety, health protection by health professionals or entities, legitimate interests of the controller or third parties, and credit protection. Legitimate interests need a balancing test, and Article 10 adds purpose, necessity, and transparency conditions. The ANPD set out its approach in a February 2024 guide. Legitimate interests cannot justify processing sensitive data. Credit protection is a standalone basis, reflecting Brazil's credit bureau ecosystem.

**After**

> Review each processing activity against the LGPD's 10 bases. Consent-based processing requires the same quality of consent as the GDPR. The credit protection basis is relevant for any organization involved in credit assessment or financial services.

### personalImpact

### professionalLayer

**Before**

> Article 18 grants data subjects in Brazil a set of rights that closely parallel GDPR Chapter III rights. The right to confirmation of processing and access to data (Article 18, I and II) mirrors GDPR Article 15. Responses must be provided in simplified or complete form as requested by the data subject. The format requirement, simplified versus complete, is an LGPD-specific nuance without direct GDPR equivalent. A simplified reply is due immediately. A complete reply is due within 15 days of the request, compared to GDPR's one-month standard. Small businesses get 15 days for a simplified reply and 30 days for a complete one, unless their processing is high-risk. Public bodies follow their own deadlines.

**After**

Small businesses get 15 days for a simplified reply and 30 days for a complete one, unless their processing is high-risk. Public bodies follow their own deadlines.

## Your Right to Remove or Restrict Your Data (`lgpd-art18-deletion`)

### personalImpact

**Before**

> Every company that keeps your data is one more place a leak can strike. Old records you forgot about still sit on their servers. Article 18 lets you ask for data that is no longer needed to be deleted. Removing it reduces how much of you is exposed.

**After**

> Every company that keeps your data is one more place a leak can strike. Old records you forgot about still sit on their servers. Article 18 lets you ask for data that is no longer needed to be deleted.

### professionalLayer

**Before**

> Article 18, IV provides three distinct remedies: anonymization, blocking, and deletion, where data is unnecessary, excessive, or processed in violation of LGPD. This is similar to GDPR's right to erasure but differs in scope. Article 18, VI separately lets data subjects ask for deletion of data processed on consent, with limited exceptions. Anonymization as a consumer remedy is an LGPD-specific option. Blocking (suspending processing) parallels GDPR's restriction of processing under Article 18. The availability of anonymization as a consumer-facing remedy creates operational complexity since true anonymization is technically demanding and irreversible. Controllers must immediately tell anyone they shared the data with, so they can repeat the correction or deletion.

**After**

> Where data is unnecessary, excessive, or processed in violation of the LGPD, Article 18, item IV, lets the person ask for anonymization, blocking, or deletion. This is similar to the GDPR's right to erasure but differs in scope. Article 18, VI separately lets data subjects ask for deletion of data processed on consent, with limited exceptions. Anonymization as a consumer remedy is an LGPD-specific option. Blocking, which suspends processing, parallels the GDPR's restriction of processing under Article 18. The availability of anonymization as a consumer-facing remedy creates operational complexity since true anonymization is technically demanding and irreversible. Controllers must immediately tell anyone they shared the data with, so they can repeat the correction or deletion.

### assessTrigger

**Before**

**After**

> Does your organization have processes for all three remedies: anonymization, blocking, and deletion, and do you have a technical definition of anonymization that meets the LGPD's standard?

## What Happens When Personal Data Is Compromised in Brazil (`lgpd-art48`)

### personalImpact

**Before**

> Given Brazil's history of large-scale leaks, a breach of your data is a real risk. The danger grows when a company stays silent about it. Article 48 requires notice to the ANPD and to you when the risk is serious. You are informed in time to protect your accounts and your CPF.

**After**

> Given Brazil's history of large-scale leaks, a breach of your data is a real risk. The danger grows when a company stays silent about it. Article 48 requires notice to the ANPD and to you when the risk is serious.

### professionalLayer

**Before**

> Article 48 requires controllers to notify the ANPD and affected data subjects of security incidents that could cause relevant harm to data subjects. LGPD does not specify a fixed notification timeframe in the legislation. ANPD Resolution CD/ANPD No. 15/2024 requires notification to the ANPD and affected data subjects within 3 business days of becoming aware of the incident, with supplementary information within 20 business days. Small processing agents, including startups and small businesses under ANPD Resolution CD/ANPD No. 2/2022, receive double the deadlines, 6 business days and 40 for the supplement, unless they carry out high-risk processing. The doubling does not apply where the incident could endanger people's physical or moral integrity. This is a materially different timeline from GDPR's 72-hour window.

**After**

> Article 48 requires controllers to notify the ANPD and affected data subjects of security incidents that could cause relevant harm to data subjects. The LGPD does not specify a fixed notification timeframe in the legislation. ANPD Resolution CD/ANPD No. 15/2024 requires notification to the ANPD and affected data subjects within three business days of becoming aware of the incident, with supplementary information within 20 business days. Small processing agents, including startups and small businesses under ANPD Resolution CD/ANPD No. 2/2022, receive double the deadlines, six business days and 40 for the supplement, unless they carry out high-risk processing. The doubling does not apply where the incident could endanger people's physical or moral integrity. This is a materially different timeline from the GDPR's 72-hour window.

> Articles 33–36

### surfaceLabel

**Before**

> Moving Personal Data Outside Brazil

**After**

> Moving Personal Data outside Brazil

### personalImpact

**Before**

> Your data often moves to servers in other countries with different rules. Without safeguards, its protection could weaken on arrival. Articles 33 to 36 require LGPD-level protection to remain in place abroad. Your safeguards travel with your data.

**After**

> Your data often moves to servers in other countries with different rules. Without safeguards, its protection could weaken on arrival. Articles 33 to 36 require LGPD-level protection to remain in place abroad.

### professionalLayer

**Before**

> Article 33 permits international transfers on nine grounds. They include countries the ANPD recognises as adequate, specific or standard contractual clauses, global corporate rules, and certifications. Others cover legal cooperation, protection of life, ANPD authorisation, public policy, and specific consent. The ANPD's standard contractual clauses took effect in August 2024, and contracts had to adopt them by August 2025. Its first adequacy decision, in January 2026, recognises the EU and EEA. The EU recognised Brazil the same day, so EU-Brazil transfers no longer need clauses. For other countries, organizations relying on standard clauses should use the ANPD's version. GDPR SCCs have no automatic recognition under LGPD.

**After**

> Article 33 permits international transfers on nine grounds. They include countries the ANPD recognizes as adequate, specific or standard contractual clauses, global corporate rules, and certifications. Others cover legal cooperation, protection of life, ANPD authorization, public policy, and specific consent. GDPR SCCs have no automatic recognition under the LGPD.
